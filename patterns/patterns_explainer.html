Background

A wordnet groups words into synsets — sets of synonyms that share one meaning. A role noun names a person by the role they fill: učitel "teacher", lékař "doctor".

Why count them? Project 1 adds feminine forms (přechylování: učitel → učitelka), and the first thing you want is the scale of the job — how many role nouns are there to work on?

The catch: the Czech wordnet is an expand wordnet. It does not store its own lexicographer files; each synset is keyed to a Princeton WordNet offset and Requires omw-en:2.0. So "is this synset about a person?" is read off the English side, two ways, and the script reports both:

  • the English lexicographer file is noun.person;
  • the synset is a transitive hyponym of person, individual, someone (offset 00007846-n).

Why two ways? Neither is the exact set of role nouns, so the script computes both and reports them side by side. When two different definitions land on nearly the same number, that agreement is reassurance.

Intuition

Every English noun synset has an offset (like 03395514-n) and a lexfile. The matching Czech synset carries the same offset. So: pick the English person-synsets, collect their offsets, and count the Czech synsets that share them.

def offset(ss_id: str) -> str:
    # ntumc-cs-03395514-n / omw-en-03395514-n -> 03395514-n
    parts = ss_id.rsplit("-", 2)
    return f"{parts[-2]}-{parts[-1]}"
