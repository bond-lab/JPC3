Background

A wordnet groups words into synsets — sets of synonyms that share one meaning. A role noun names a person by the role they fill: učitel "teacher", lékař "doctor", pekař "baker", vědec "scientist", právník "lawyer", knihovník "librarian", herec "actor" — all of them synsets the Czech wordnet already has.

Why count them? Project 1 adds feminine forms (přechylování: učitel → učitelka), and the first thing you want is the scale of the job — how many role nouns are there to work on? The data shows what "done" looks like, and what is missing. The teacher synset (i93557) already holds učitel, instruktor, učitelka; neighbor holds soused, sousedka; proletarian holds dělník, dělnice, proletář. But doctor (i89560) holds only lékař, doktor, scientist only vědec, and lawyer has five lemmas from právník to obhájce, none of them feminine — no lékařka, vědkyně, právnička. A crude count (a lemma ending in -ka, -ice or -yně beside another lemma) finds only about 70 of the 1,922 person synsets with a feminine form already in place; the rest are the pool to work on.

The catch: the Czech WordNet (release 2.2) has no lexicographer files, and it records relations one way only, so a synset knows its hypernym but not its hyponyms. What it does have is an ILI, an identifier from the Collaborative Interlingual Index, on 28,491 of its 28,977 synsets, and the English wordnet omw-en:2.0 carries the same ILIs. So "is this synset about a person?" is read off the English side, two ways, and the script reports both:

  • the English lexicographer file is noun.person;
  • the synset is a transitive hyponym of person, individual, someone (ILI i35562) — for teacher the chain is person → adult → professional → educator → teacher, four steps down.

What does noun.person actually hold? Far more than role nouns. Here is what the Czech wordnet has in it, by kind:

English has Einstein, Shakespeare and Comenius in noun.person too, but the Czech wordnet has no synset with their ILIs, so they are not counted.

Why two ways? Neither is the exact set of role nouns, so the script computes both and reports them side by side. When two different definitions land on nearly the same number, that agreement is reassurance.

Intuition

Every English noun synset has an ILI (like i35562) and a lexfile. The matching Czech synset carries the same ILI. So: pick the English person-synsets, collect their ILIs, and count the Czech synsets that share them. Not their ids: the Czech ids are Princeton 2.0 offsets (wncze-02001223-n), the English ones 3.0, and only 124 of 28,721 coincide.

The gap between them is small and, once you look, explainable. 1,849 Czech synsets are in both sets. Of the 73 that are in noun.person only, 56 are imaginary or supernatural beings — mořská panna "mermaid", vlkodlak "werewolf", anděl "angel", kentaur "centaur", obr "giant", smrtka "Death" — which Princeton files under noun.person but hangs below imaginary being or spiritual being, not person. The other 17 hang below causal agent or organism: šofér "chauffeur" (a driver, which is an operator), maskot "mascot", osud "fate". The one synset in the closure only is person itself, whose lexfile is noun.Tops.

About 1,900 role-noun synsets, against 11,087 noun.person synsets on the English side — roughly 17% coverage. noun.person is an upper bound: it also holds nationalities (Kubánec), kinship terms (matka), named individuals (Adam, Achilles) and people by trait (zbabělec "coward"), so the motion-eligible subset is smaller.

2 · english_person_ilis()

count_role_nouns.py:43–64

Build the two English target sets. The first is a filter on the lexfile. The second walks the hyponym closure of person with an explicit stack (a depth-first search), guarding against cycles with a seen set.

by_lexfile = {ss.ili for ss in en.synsets(pos="n")
              if ss.ili and ss.lexfile() == "noun.person"}
...
stack, seen = [root], set()
while stack:
    cur = stack.pop()
    ...
    if cur.ili:
        closure.add(cur.ili)
    stack.extend(cur.hyponyms())

The walk runs on the English side on purpose: the Czech WordNet stores hypernym links only, so hyponyms() on a Czech synset returns nothing.

Concretely, the lexfile set has 11,087 ILIs and the closure 10,297. The walk starts at person, pushes its direct hyponyms (adult, worker, leader, expert, … about 400 of them), and keeps popping until every descendant — down to governess under teacher — has been seen once. 3 · czech_counts()

count_role_nouns.py:67–74

Given a set of target ILIs, count the Czech synsets whose ili is in it, the distinct lemmas across them, and the lemma-synset pairs (senses).

syns = [ss for ss in cs_nouns if ss.ili in target]

The three numbers differ because a synset can hold several lemmas and a lemma can sit in several synsets. Two real child synsets show both at once:

i88964  child, kid, youngster   capart, dítě, špunt, děcko
i88965  child, kid              prcek, dítě, děcko

synsets 2 · distinct lemmas 5 (dítě and děcko appear in both) · senses 7

On the whole wordnet that is why 1,922 synsets give 2,581 lemmas but 3,117 senses.

Glossary

TermWhat it means here
synseta set of synonyms sharing one meaning — the unit a wordnet counts.
lemmaa word form in a synset (učitel).
ILIan identifier like i35562 from the Collaborative Interlingual Index, one per concept across all wordnets; the key that matches English and Czech synsets.
offseta Princeton WordNet synset id like 02001223-n; it changes between WordNet versions (the Czech ids are 2.0, the English 3.0), so it is not a safe key.
lexicographer filea coarse English sense class (noun.person, noun.artifact, …).
expand wordneta wordnet built by translating the English synsets, so its concepts line up with English and share ILIs; the Czech WordNet is one.
